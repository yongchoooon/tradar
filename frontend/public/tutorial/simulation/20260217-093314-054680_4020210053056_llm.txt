*   The Compared prior mark "PIXEL RADAR" conveys the concept of a radar operating at a pixel level or within a digital/imaging context.

*   The User mark designates "trademark information retrieval research" (Class 45) and "information retrieval services on the internet" (Class 35).

Consumers are likely to believe that "T-RADAR" retrieval services and "PIXEL RADAR" identification software originate from the same entity due to the functional convergence of IT services and software products.

## 1. Rebuttal of Similarity between Marks

### 1. Dissimilarity in Appearance and Visual Composition
*   The User mark "T-RADAR" is a compact, hyphenated single-word structure consisting of only seven characters. In contrast, the Prior mark "PIXEL RADAR" is composed of two distinct English words separated by a space, creating a significantly different visual rhythm and length.
*   The letter 'T' in the User mark serves as a prominent, stylized prefix that immediately distinguishes it from the multi-syllable word "PIXEL." Consumers are trained to focus on the prefix of a mark, and the visual brevity of "T-" compared to "PIXEL" prevents any likelihood of visual confusion.

### 2. Phonetic Distinction and Initial Sound Importance
*   Phonetically, the User mark "T-RADAR" (티-레이더) consists of four syllables in Korean pronunciation, whereas the Prior mark "PIXEL RADAR" (픽셀 레이더) consists of six syllables. This difference in syllable count and cadence creates a clear phonetic distinction.
*   In trademark jurisprudence, the initial sound of a mark is generally considered the most critical factor in consumer recognition. The sharp, single-syllable "T" (티) sound is entirely different from the two-syllable "PIX-EL" (픽셀) sound. As the prefixes are distinct, the overall phonetic impression of the marks remains dissimilar even if they share a common suffix.

### 3. Weak Distinctiveness of the Term "RADAR"
*   The examiner's argument relies heavily on the shared word "RADAR." However, in the context of "information retrieval" (User mark) and "sensing/detection software" (Prior mark), the term "RADAR" is highly suggestive, if not descriptive, of the function of searching, scanning, or detecting data.
*   Because "RADAR" describes a core characteristic of the designated goods and services, its ability to function as a source identifier is weak. Therefore, the scope of protection for "RADAR" should be limited, and the focus of comparison should shift to the distinctive prefixes "T" and "PIXEL," which are unmistakably different.

## 2. Rebuttal of Similarity between Goods and Services

### 1. Divergence in Target Consumers and Purpose
*   The User mark's services, such as "trademark information retrieval research" (Class 45), are highly specialized professional services targeted at legal experts, trademark attorneys, and corporate IP departments. 
*   The Prior mark's goods in Class 09, such as "electronic sensors," "biometric authentication software," and "motion recognition sensors," are technical hardware components or security software used by engineers, security professionals, or hardware manufacturers. 
*   Given the specialized nature of these fields, the relevant consumers are sophisticated and likely to exercise a high degree of care, further reducing any possibility of confusion between a professional IP service and a technical sensor component.

### 2. Distinction between Professional Services and Industrial Hardware
*   While the examiner suggests "functional convergence" between software and services, there is a fundamental difference between providing "trademark research" (a service involving analysis and reporting) and "sensors/biometric software" (industrial or security hardware/software). 
*   The User mark does not involve the sale of software as a product but provides a specialized information service. Therefore, there is no direct competitive relationship or overlap in the channels of trade with the goods of the Prior mark.

## 3. Proposed Adjustment Directions

### 1. Narrowing the Scope of Designated Services
*   To further distinguish the User mark from the Prior mark’s technical and security-focused software, the applicant may consider refining the descriptions of services in Class 35 and 45. 
*   For example, specifying the services as "trademark legal information retrieval" or "business information research for intellectual property" would clarify that the User mark operates in a professional legal/business domain, distinctly separate from the electronic sensor and security software industry of Class 09.

### 2. Emphasizing the "T" Element
*   The applicant can argue that "T" specifically stands for "Trademark" or "Technology" in a way that creates a specific compound meaning with "RADAR," distinguishing it from the "PIXEL" (digital imaging) concept of the Prior mark. This conceptual divergence ensures that consumers perceive the marks as representing entirely different fields of expertise.
